# FAO Design Guidelines (English Units)

Core design rules drawn from FAO *Wood Gas as Engine Fuel* (1986) and related SERI practice, converted to US customary units.

## Gas Yield
Approximate yield: **35–40 scf of producer gas per pound of dry wood**.

## Hearth Load / Superficial Velocity (Imbert throat)
- Maximum continuous: ~2.95 scf/h per in² of throat area (approaches high SV)
- Minimum for low tar: ~1.0–1.15 scf/h per in²
- Typical design point: ~2.3–2.6 scf/h per in² historically; modern DIY often ~1.0–1.3 for ~1.25 m/s
- Turndown ratio usually 2.5–3

## Throat Diameter
Throat area (in²) = gas flow (scf/h) ÷ B_g
d_t (inches) = √(4 × Area / π)

## Geometry Rules
- Nozzle air velocity: 100–115 ft/s
- Throat angle: 45–60°
- Hearth diameter at air inlet: throat + ~4 in (single) or ~8 in (double/Imbert)
- Reduction zone height: minimum 8 in (12–16 in common)
- Nozzle plane above throat: start ~4 in (typically 0.9–1.6 × throat diameter)
- Air requirement: 1.0–1.5 lb air per lb dry wood (ER ≈ 0.25–0.35)

## Hopper
Size for desired run time. Bulk density of wood chips/blocks typically 9–19 lb/ft³. Keep particle size uniform (¾–2 in) to reduce bridging.

## Engine Notes
- Expect 20–40% derate vs gasoline
- Clean, cool, dry gas required
- Match gasifier output to engine mixture airflow
